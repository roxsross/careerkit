# Workflow: career transition

Respond with care, without assuming the user has time, money, or emotional bandwidth. Do not promise employment or use layoffs as marketing hooks.

1. **Immediate needs:** If the user is newly laid off or suspended, prioritize document preservation, clarity about benefits/terms, financial triage and local qualified advice before signing any agreement. Do not give individualized legal rulings.
2. **Experience inventory:** Identify verified accomplishments, transferable skills, work samples and recommendations. Respect confidentiality.
3. **Focus:** Select a small set of realistic target roles and job sources; compare requirements against demonstrated skills.
4. **Materials:** Prioritize CV, LinkedIn, portfolio and tailored applications. A good small project beats speculative credentials.
5. **People:** Suggest outreach to former colleagues, communities and recruiters; do not require public disclosure of personal circumstances.
6. **Cadence:** Make a sustainable five- or seven-day plan with rest and a reasonable application tracking method.
7. **Review:** Reassess response quality and effort after one or two weeks; never promise a conversion rate.

Use `templates/career-plan.md`. Offer wording that is ready to send only when requested.
